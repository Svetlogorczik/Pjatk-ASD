## The system in short

Passing is **points-based** — you collect points from four sources, and attendance is compulsory on top of that. The elements are "stepped": you are admitted to the next one only after passing the previous ones.

| # | Element | Max | Threshold | Admission condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Attendance** at classes | — | max **2** unexcused absences | — |
| 1 | **Entry quizzes** (*wejściówki*) — short quizzes at the start of (almost) every class, on the **previous lecture** | **12 pts** (12 × 1 pt) | min **5 pts** in total | — |
| 2 | **Practical test** — about 10 tasks, mainly on knowing how algorithms work; end of semester, during classes, 2 dates | **20 pts** | min **10 pts** | passed 0) and 1) |
| 3 | **Knowledge/skills test** — definitions, concepts, complexities, specifications, code analysis, simple pseudocode, applying the algorithms to simple problems; after the practical test, 2 dates | **20 pts** | min **10 pts** | passed 0), 1) and 2) |
| 4 | **Activity** — the teacher gives a task, everyone solves it alone for a few minutes, a volunteer presents the solution at the board | about **7 pts** (max 1 pt per task) | — | — |

:::warn Watch out
- **More than 2 unexcused absences** (an excuse is e.g. a medical certificate) = no admission to the other elements and **failing** the course.
- **Entry quizzes cannot be retaken.** Every missed or unprepared quiz is a point lost for good — and the threshold is 5 out of 12.
- A quiz is about the **previous lecture** — after every lecture, go through the slides and the summary of the matching topic.
:::

### Scope of the practical test

Orders of functions / O notation (and related), **binSearch**, **selection sort**, **insertion sort**, **mergeSort**, **quickSort / partition**, **countSort**, **radixSort**, **minHeap** (binary min-heap), **BST**, **in/pre/post-order** traversals, **BFS/DFS**, **Kruskal**.

Typical task wording, sample data with step-by-step solutions and practice tasks: [Tests 2026/2027](page:exams).

### Scope of the knowledge/skills test

Definitions and concepts, complexities, specifications and properties of the algorithms and data structures covered, **code analysis**, **writing simple pseudocode**, the ability to **apply** the algorithms/data structures (or simple modifications of them) to simple computational problems. The lecturer's hints on what to focus on: [Tests 2026/2027 → knowledge test](page:exams).

## Class grade

**Grade = (points collected) / 10, rounded down to the nearest grade.** Example from the announcement: 47 pts → 4.7 → **4.5**.

| Points | Grade |
|---|---|
| 30–34 | 3.0 |
| 35–39 | 3.5 |
| 40–44 | 4.0 |
| 45–49 | 4.5 |
| 50 or more | 5.0 |

:::own
The site author derived this table from the rule "points / 10, rounded down to the nearest grade". In total you can collect about **59 pts** (12 + 20 + 20 + about 7). Note: the minimum thresholds alone give 5 + 10 + 10 = **25 pts**, i.e. 2.5 — which, rounded down, is not yet 3.0. So according to the table you need **30 pts** for a 3.0; if this matters to you, confirm with the lecturer.
:::

### Example of counting points {own}

:::own
An example by the site author.
:::

Entry quizzes: 8 pts (≥ 5 ✓). Practical test: 15 pts (≥ 10 ✓). Knowledge test: 13 pts (≥ 10 ✓). Activity: 3 pts.

Total **39 pts** → 3.9 → **3.5**. One more point (e.g. one activity at the board) would give 40 pts → **4.0**.

## Lecture grade

- There are **two grades**: for the classes and for the lecture.
- The class grade can be **transferred** to the lecture grade — **up to 4.0 at most**.
- If you want **4.5 or 5.0** for the lecture, you can take an **additional exam** (written/oral) that checks **understanding** of the material. **The grade from this exam is final** (so it can also be lower than the transferred one).

## A plan for the semester {own}

:::own
Recommendations by the site author — not part of the rules.
:::

1. **Don't skip classes** — the limit is only 2 unexcused absences.
2. **After every lecture** (at the latest the day before the class) go through the slides: the definitions in the "Definition" boxes and the list "What you must know" — that is how you collect entry-quiz points.
3. From mid-semester, **practise tracing algorithms by hand** from the practical-test scope — the [Tests 2026/2027](page:exams) page has ready-made sets with solutions. Full mock tests and entry quizzes: [Practice tests](page:mock).
4. Volunteer at the board — **activity** points can't be lost.
5. Before the knowledge test, learn the **exact definitions by heart** (correctness, complexity, notations) — see topics [2](topic:t02) and [3](topic:t03), sections "2026/2027 lecture version".
